# 03 — الاسْمُ وعَلاماتُه (The Noun and its Signs)

> **Video:** [الاسم وعلاماته](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IaDb6BMI9m4)
> **Topic:** How to *recognise* a noun — its definition and its **علامات** (the signs/tests that prove a word is an اسم).

---

## Definition

**الاسْم**: a word that denotes a meaning **with no time attached to it** (لا يَقترن بزمان).
- Contrast with the **فِعل**, whose meaning is **bound to a time** (past/present/etc.).
- So "time-independence" is the conceptual mark of the noun; the items below are the *outward* tests.

---

## The signs of a noun (علامات الاسم)

If a word can take any of these, it is an **اسم**:

### 1. الجَرّ / الخَفْض (al-jarr / al-khafḍ) — *being in the genitive*
A noun can be **مَجْرور**.
- **بِسْمِ اللَّهِ** — *bismi-llāhi*: `اسم` and `الله` are مجرور (kasrah). Only nouns do this.
- ⚠️ Subtlety: the *sign* is **being in a state of jarr**, not merely "a particle stands before it." (A حرف like اللام can appear before a verb — e.g. **لِيَفْعَل** *li-yafʿal*, the "lām of command" — without making it a noun, because that lām is not the genitive operator on it.)

### 2. التَّنْوين (at-tanwīn) — *nunation*
A noun can carry **tanwīn** ( ـٌ ـٍ ـً ).
- **رَجُلٌ، كِتابٍ، قَلَمًا** — the doubled-vowel ending is tanwīn; verbs and particles don't take it.
- 📌 *Aside (rare):* there are several **types of tanwīn**; one special poetic kind is **تَنْوين التَّرَنُّم** — a tanwīn added at line-ends in poetry purely for **rhythm/rhyme (الإطلاق)**, not as a true noun-sign.
  - Cited verse (jarīr): **أَقِلِّي اللَّوْمَ عاذِلَ والعِتابَنْ** — "Lessen the blame, O blaming woman, and the reproach." Here **عاذِل** = the blaming/criticising woman, and the final *-n* is poetic ترنّم, not ordinary tanwīn. (The real noun-sign is the *ordinary* tanwīn; the types are detailed later in their own chapter.)

### 3. النِّداء (an-nidāʾ) — *being called/vocative*
A noun can be **called** with a vocative particle (يا، أ ...).
- **يا زَيْدُ** — you can only "call" a noun. So if **يا** can precede it meaningfully, it's an اسم.
- ⚠️ Subtlety: a **يا** is not always vocative — e.g. **يا لَيْتَ قَوْمي يَعْلَمونَ** (Qurʾān, *Yā-Sīn* 36:26): here **يا** *emphasises* the wish (it stretches/heightens the longing of لَيْتَ), it is **not** calling anyone. So a يا on a word is only a noun-sign when it is **genuinely vocative**.

### 4. دُخول الألف واللام (الـ / أل) — *taking the definite article*
A noun can accept **أل**.
- **الرَّجُل، الكِتاب** — only nouns take أل.
- ⚠️ Subtlety: **أل entering doesn't mean it just "sits before" the word — it must actually make it definite.** A لام that attaches to a *verb* (e.g. لام of command on **لِيَشهَد / يَشهَد**) is **not** أل and does not make the word a noun.

### 5. الإسْناد إليه (al-isnād ilayhi) — *being the thing predicated about*
A noun can occupy a slot where something is **predicated of it** — i.e. it can be a **مُبتدأ** (subject of a nominal sentence), a **فاعل** (doer), etc.
- **زَيْدٌ قائمٌ** — *Zayd is standing*: **زيد** is the مبتدأ (the thing we speak *about*). Only a noun fills this slot.

## Scholarly asides (general knowledge — not parsing-critical)

### On أل: is it **أل** or just **اللام**?
There's a known disagreement (خِلاف) about the definite article:
- **سِيبَوَيْه (Sībawayh):** the article is really just the **اللام**; the alif (hamzah) before it is only a **هَمْزة وَصْل** added so you can start pronouncing it (you can't begin with a sākin lām). So the *true* particle = **ل**.
- **الخَليل (al-Khalīl):** it's **أل** — hamzah and lām **together**.
- **المُبَرِّد:** the lām differentiates definiteness, with his own formulation (in his book **المُقتَضَب**).
- Practically, we just say **"أل"** (the two together). The خلاف is good to recognise when you see different books use different terms.

### Who was Sībawayh?
He began as a **student of ḥadīth**, kept slipping in the **iʿrāb** (case-endings) of words, so he paused ḥadīth to master the **language** first — and became the "godfather of naḥw." His masterwork is known simply as **الكِتاب** (al-Kitāb): so famous that when grammarians say **"كِتابُه" / "الكِتاب,"** they mean *his* book.

## Quick reference: علامات الاسم

| # | Sign (Arabic) | Test | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | الجَرّ / الخَفْض | can be genitive | بِسْمِ **اللَّهِ** |
| 2 | التَّنْوين | can take nunation | رَجُل**ٌ** |
| 3 | النِّداء | can be called | **يا** زَيْدُ |
| 4 | أل (الألف واللام) | can take the article | **ال**رَّجُل |
| 5 | الإسْناد إليه | can be subject/مبتدأ/فاعل | **زَيْدٌ** قائمٌ |

> Memory aid: **a word is a noun if it can be made genitive, take tanwīn, be called, take "al-", or be the thing you speak about.**
