# 02 — مُقَدِّمَةُ الكِتاب (Introduction): اللفظ، القول، الكلمة

> **Video:** [مقدمة الكتاب](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gj7Ko3hORDs)
> **Topic:** The foundational vocabulary of grammar — what an *utterance*, a *meaningful word*, and *speech* are, and how words divide into **مُفْرَد** (single) vs **مُرَكَّب** (compound), ending with the three kinds of word.

---

## The ladder of terms (from broadest to narrowest)

Each term narrows the one before it:

### 1. اللَّفْظ (al-lafẓ) — *utterance / sound*
Anything that comes out of the mouth as sound — **whether or not it means anything**.
- Snoring, a baby's babbling — all of it is **لَفْظ**.

اللفظ splits into two:

| Type | Arabic | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Meaningless | **المُهْمَل** (al-muhmal) | a sound with no assigned meaning — e.g. a baby's babble |
| Meaningful | **المَوْضُوع** (al-mawḍūʿ) | a sound **coined for a meaning** (وُضِعَ لِمَعْنًى) |

> **وَضْع** = the act of assigning a sound to a meaning. A مَوْضوع word is one "laid down" to denote something.

### 2. القَوْل (al-qawl) — *meaningful utterance*
A **لفظ that denotes a meaning** (لفظٌ دالٌّ على معنًى).
- **زيد** denotes a person, **قَلَم** denotes a pen, **وَرَق** denotes paper. Each is a قَوْل.
- A baby's babble is **not** a قول (no meaning).

---

## القَوْل divides by structure: مُفْرَد vs مُرَكَّب

### المُفْرَد (al-mufrad) — *single / simple*
A word whose **individual parts do NOT denote part of the meaning**.
- **زَيْدٌ** — the letters ز / ي / د carry no separate meanings of their own; only the whole denotes the person.
- A name like **جَعْفَر** — even though it has several letters, no letter or syllable is itself a meaningful piece.

### المُرَكَّب (al-murakkab) — *compound*
A word/phrase whose **parts each denote a separate meaning**.
- **عَبْدُ اللَّهِ** — **عَبْد** ("slave") means something *and* **اللَّه** ("God") means something; the parts each carry meaning.

> ⚠️ **Two different meanings of "مُفْرَد" — don't confuse them:**
> 1. **In number (العَدَد):** مُفْرَد = **singular** (وَاحِد), opposite of **المُثَنّى** (dual) and **الجَمْع** (plural).
> 2. **In structure (التركيب):** مُفْرَد = a **single word**, opposite of **المُرَكَّب** (a phrase/compound).
> So "مفرد" can mean *one in number* OR *not compound*, depending on context.

The مُرَكَّب itself can be:
- a **phrase** (a مركّب that is not yet a full sentence), or
- a complete **جُمْلة** (jumlah) — a full sentence.

---

## القَوْل / الكَلِمة divides by kind: the three classes of word

The meaningful single word — **الكَلِمة** — is of **three types** only:

| # | Arabic | Translit. | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **الاسْم** | al-ism | noun |
| 2 | **الفِعْل** | al-fiʿl | verb |
| 3 | **الحَرْف** | al-ḥarf | particle |

> **الكَلِمةُ ثَلاثةُ أقسامٍ: اسمٌ وفِعلٌ وحَرفٌ.**

Each of these three gets its own treatment in the videos that follow (starting with **الاسم**).

---

## Quick map

```
اللفظ (any utterance)
├── مُهْمَل  (meaningless)
└── مَوْضوع → القَوْل (meaningful)
        ├── by structure:  مُفْرَد  ↔  مُرَكَّب (phrase / جملة)
        └── by kind (الكلمة):  اسم — فِعل — حَرف
```

## Why bother with these terms?
Many won't change how you parse the Qurʾān directly, but they recur constantly in the grammar books (كتب النحو). Knowing them means that when a text says *لفظ*, *مهمل*, *موضوع*, *مفرد*, *مركّب*, you already know exactly what it's pointing at.
