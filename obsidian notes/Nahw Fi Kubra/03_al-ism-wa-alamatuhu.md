# 03 — الاسْمُ وعَلاماتُه (The Noun and its Signs)

> **Video:** [الاسم وعلاماته](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IaDb6BMI9m4)
> **Book:** القسم الأول: الاسم — علاماته (p.6–7)
> **Topic:** How to *recognise* a noun — its definition and its **علامات** (the tests that prove a word is an اسم).

---

## Definition

> **الاسْمُ: ما دَلَّ على معنًى في نفسِه غيرَ مُقترنٍ بزمانٍ.**

A word that denotes a meaning **with no time attached** (لا يقترن بزمان) — contrast the **[[02_muqaddimah|فِعل]]**, whose meaning is **bound to a time**. "Time-independence" is the conceptual mark; the items below are the *outward* tests.

---

## The signs of a noun (علامات الاسم)

If a word can take any of these, it is an **اسم**:

### 1. الجَرّ / الخَفْض (al-jarr / al-khafḍ) — *being in the genitive*
A noun can be **مَجْرور**.
- **بِسْمِ اللَّهِ** — `اسم` and `الله` are مجرور (kasrah). Only nouns do this.
- ⚠️ The *sign* is **being in a state of jarr**, not merely "a particle stands before it" (the لام in **لِيَفْعَل** *li-yafʿal* precedes a verb without making it a noun).

### 2. التَّنْوين (at-tanwīn) — *nunation*
A noun can carry **tanwīn** ( ـٌ ـٍ ـً ): **هُدًى لِلْمُتَّقِينَ**, **رَجُلٌ**, **قَلَمًا**.
- 📌 *Aside:* one special poetic type is **تَنْوين التَّرَنُّم** — added at line-ends purely for **rhythm/rhyme (الإطلاق)**, not as a true noun-sign.
  - Cited verse: **أَقِلِّي اللَّوْمَ عاذِلَ والعِتابا** — "Lessen the blame, O blaming woman, and the reproach." **عاذِل** = the blaming woman; the final tone is poetic ترنّم.

### 3. النِّداء (an-nidāʾ) — *being called/vocative*
A noun can be **called**: **يا زَيْدُ** (book ex.: **يُنْوَحُ / يا...**).
- ⚠️ **يا** is not always vocative — **يا لَيْتَ قَوْمي يَعْلَمونَ** (Yā-Sīn 36:26): here **يا** *emphasises* the wish (لَيْتَ), it is **not** calling anyone. Only a *genuine* vocative is a noun-sign.

### 4. دُخول أل (الألف واللام) — *taking the definite article*
A noun can accept **أل**: **الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ**.
- ⚠️ أل must actually make the word **definite** — a لām attaching to a *verb* is not أل.

### 5. الإسْناد إليه (al-isnād ilayhi) — *being predicated about*
A noun can be the thing something is predicated of — **مُبتدأ**, **فاعل**, etc.
- **زَيْدٌ قائمٌ** — *Zayd is standing*: **زيد** is the مبتدأ. Book ex.: **إنَّ إبراهيمَ لحليمٌ أوّاهٌ مُنِيبٌ** (إبراهيم is مسند إليه).

> Book also lists **being مُضاف**: **رَبِّ العالَمِينَ** (a noun can be the مضاف). And: **الفعلُ والحرفُ لا يكونان مجرورين** — verbs and particles are never genitive.

## Scholarly asides (general knowledge — not parsing-critical)

### On أل: is it **أل** or just **اللام**?
A known disagreement (خِلاف), noted in the book's fawāʾid (p.7):
- **سِيبَوَيْه (d.180 AH):** the article is just the **اللام**; the hamzah is only a **هَمْزة وَصْل** added so you can begin pronouncing the sākin lām.
- **الخَليل (d.170 AH):** it's **أل** — hamzah + lām **together**.
- **المُبَرِّد (d.285 AH):** the hamzah is a **همزة مفتوحة** that is the article, with the lām to differentiate it from the hamzah of istifhām.
- Practically we just say **"أل."**

### Who was Sībawayh?
He began as a **student of ḥadīth**, kept slipping in the **iʿrāb** of words, so he turned to mastering the **language** — and became the giant of naḥw. His masterwork is known simply as **الكِتاب**: when grammarians say **"كِتابُه,"** they mean *his* book.

## Quick reference: علامات الاسم

| # | Sign | Test | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | الجَرّ / الخَفْض | can be genitive | بِسْمِ **اللَّهِ** |
| 2 | التَّنْوين | can take nunation | هُدًى لِلْمُتَّقِينَ |
| 3 | النِّداء | can be called | **يا** زَيْدُ |
| 4 | أل | can take the article | **ال**رَّحْمٰن |
| 5 | الإسْناد إليه (+ الإضافة) | subject / مضاف | **زَيْدٌ** قائمٌ · رَبِّ العالَمِين |

The matn line by line, with translation:

![[n03-l1.png]]
> *The first kind: the noun (al-ism).*

![[n03-l2.png]]
> *It is that which denotes a meaning in itself, not coupled with a time.*

![[n03-l3.png]]
> **[heading]** *Signs of the noun (ʿalāmāt al-ism).*

![[n03-l4.png]]
> *The noun is recognised…*

![[n03-l5.png]]
> *…by its being genitive (مَجْرورًا) — e.g. ﴿bismi **llāhi**﴾ (in the name of Allah).*

![[n03-l6.png]]
> *…and by [taking] "al-" for definiteness — e.g. ﴿**ar-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm**﴾ (the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate).*

![[n03-l7.png]]
> *…and by [taking] tanwīn — e.g. ﴿hudan **li-l-muttaqīn**﴾ (a guidance for the God-conscious).*

![[n03-l8.png]]
> *…and by being predicated upon (مُسْنَدًا إليه) — e.g. ﴿inna **Ibrāhīma** la-ḥalīmun awwāhun munīb﴾ (indeed Ibrāhīm was forbearing, tender-hearted, oft-returning).*

![[n03-l9.png]]
> *…and by being a vocative (مُنادًى) — e.g. ﴿**yā Nūḥu**﴾ (O Noah).*

![[n03-l10.png]]
> *…and by being a possessive head (مُضافًا) — e.g. ﴿**rabbi** l-ʿālamīn﴾ (Lord of the worlds).*

![[n03-l11.png]]
> *The verb and the particle are never genitive, even if a preposition enters upon them — e.g. ﴿li-yakūna li-l-ʿālamīna **nadhīrā**﴾ (that he be a warner to the worlds), and ﴿wa-shhad bi-**annā** muslimūn﴾ (and bear witness that we are Muslims).*

![[n03-l12.png]]
> *The vocative particles may enter upon a non-vocative — e.g. ﴿**yā layta** qawmī yaʿlamūn﴾ (oh, would that my people knew!) — here "yā" is for emphasis, not calling.*
